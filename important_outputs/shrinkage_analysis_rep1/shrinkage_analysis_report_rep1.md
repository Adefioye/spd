## Executive Summary

The clearest conclusion from this `rep1` sweep is that the shrinkage story should **not** be framed as raw decomposition norms collapsing. Across all ten runs, the raw SPD layer reconstructions remain extremely close to target scale at the final checkpoint. The main shrinkage signal instead appears in two other places:

1. **CI attenuation is large and persistent**, especially in `linear1`, and more broadly across hidden layers in deeper untied models.
2. **Paper-style shrinkage, defined by high MMCS with low ML2R, does appear**, but it is selective. It is strongest and most trustworthy in the deepest untied run (`6L untied`) and in a smaller tied case (`5L tied linear1`).

So the high-level answer is:

- there is **little evidence for raw under-scaling**
- there is **strong evidence for CI-driven attenuation**
- there is **real ML2R shrinkage in specific matched layers**
- the strongest concentration of trustworthy shrinkage is in **deep untied runs**, especially `6L untied`
- some of the apparent deepest tied anomalies are **not simple shrinkage**, but rather overshoot or matching pathologies

## Important Caveat About Functional Ratios

Some output-ratio and Jacobian-ratio plots are numerically unstable because the denominator norms for the target can become extremely small on the probe set. This creates astronomically large ratios even when absolute MSE remains small.

Two especially important unstable cases are:

- `6L tied`: huge raw output / Jacobian ratios despite small output MSE
- `5L untied`: huge masked output ratio despite small masked output MSE

These should **not** be interpreted as evidence of literal billion-scale contraction or expansion. For functional interpretation, the most trustworthy signals here are:

- output MSE
- stable ratio cases where the denominator is not near zero
- broad comparative trends, not the raw magnitude of the unstable spikes

## 1. Raw Under-Scaling: Almost Entirely Absent

### Result

Raw layer Frobenius ratios at the final checkpoint span:

- minimum: `0.9923`
- maximum: `1.0426`

No layer in any run ever crossed the onset threshold:

- raw under-scaling defined as `raw_fro_ratio < 0.90`

The `raw_under_scaling_onset.csv` table is entirely empty for that threshold.

### Interpretation

This is the most important negative result in the whole analysis.

If the working hypothesis had been that deeper or untied models simply learn shrunken raw SPD factors, the current `rep1` data does not support it. The raw component reconstructions are generally very close to target scale, often slightly above 1.0 rather than below it.

### Visual support

The clearest support comes from:

- `plots/raw_delta/raw_under_scaling_depth6_untied.png`
- `plots/summary/layer1_summary_heatmap_tied.png`
- `plots/summary/layer1_summary_heatmap_untied.png`

In those figures, the raw under-scaling channel stays near zero across almost all conditions.

### Consequence for the shrinkage question

This means the core shrinkage phenomenon is **not** well-described as:

- “the learned `U,V` factors become too small”

Instead, anything that looks like shrinkage must be coming from:

- post-factor CI masking
- residual redistribution into delta
- post-hoc matching behavior
- or a combination of these

## 2. Delta Residual Usage: A Secondary But Important Compensatory Channel

### Result

The largest final delta Frobenius ratios occur in untied and deeper runs:

| Architecture | Depth | Layer | Final delta Fro ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| untied | 5 | linear2 | 0.2808 |
| untied | 6 | linear1 | 0.2140 |
| untied | 4 | linear2 | 0.1572 |
| untied | 6 | hidden_layers.2 | 0.1358 |
| untied | 3 | linear2 | 0.1279 |
| untied | 6 | hidden_layers.3 | 0.1114 |
| untied | 6 | linear2 | 0.1081 |
| tied | 6 | linear1 / linear2 | 0.0736 |

By contrast, tied `2L` to `4L` runs have tiny delta usage, around a few thousandths.

### Interpretation

This does not mean the decomposition has failed. It means some runs are increasingly relying on the delta residual to carry unresolved structure. That pattern is much stronger in:

- untied runs
- deeper runs

This is consistent with the idea that:

- deeper untied models preserve raw layer scale reasonably well
- but do so partly by offloading unresolved structure into delta rather than into clean matched subcomponents

The strongest examples are `5L untied linear2` and `6L untied linear1`.

### Relevance to shrinkage

Delta usage matters because it weakens any simplistic interpretation of low ML2R as “the layer is globally too small.” A layer can have:

- raw norm close to target
- low ML2R for matched subcomponents
- substantial delta residual

That combination suggests the run may have the right total scale while storing it in a way that does not map neatly onto the paper’s matched-component notion of recovery.

| tied | 3 | 1.003 | 0.630 | 0.373 |

This is a strong result. For `linear1`, the model is not primarily shrinking the raw learned weight. Instead, it is learning a raw layer of roughly correct scale and then running it through a CI regime that removes roughly `30%` to `41%` of the norm on average.

Untied runs generally show stronger `linear1` attenuation than tied runs, especially at `3L` and `4L`.

## 4. ML2R Shrinkage: Real, Selective, And Concentrated In Deep Untied Models

### Definition used

Following the SPD paper framing, shrinkage is interpreted through:

- high directional recovery: high MMCS
- low magnitude recovery: ML2R below 1

For stronger claims in this report, I use:

- trustworthy paper-style shrinkage: `MMCS >= 0.95` and `ML2R < 0.90`

### Final `linear1` summary

| Architecture | Depth | MMCS | ML2R | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tied | 2 | 1.000 | 0.991 | no meaningful shrinkage |
| tied | 3 | 1.000 | 1.000 | no shrinkage |
| tied | 4 | 1.000 | 1.011 | slight overshoot |
| tied | 5 | 0.985 | 0.891 | clear shrinkage |
| tied | 6 | 0.993 | 9.064 | overshoot / pathology, not shrinkage |
| untied | 2 | 0.999 | 0.884 | clear shrinkage |
| untied | 3 | 0.976 | 0.920 | mild-to-moderate shrinkage |
| untied | 4 | 0.872 | 0.637 | low ML2R but MMCS too low for a clean shrinkage claim |
| untied | 5 | 0.983 | 1.055 | overshoot rather than shrinkage |
| untied | 6 | 0.974 | 0.702 | strong shrinkage |

### Interpretation

The layer-1 result is not monotonic in depth, but it is still very informative.

For tied runs:

- `2L` to `4L` look strong and well-calibrated
- `5L` is the clearest tied shrinkage case
- `6L` is not a simple continuation of `5L`; it flips into a high-MMCS, extreme-ML2R overshoot regime

For untied runs:

- `2L` already shows real shrinkage
- `3L` still looks shrunken but less severely
- `4L` has low ML2R but also weak MMCS, so interpretation is ambiguous
- `5L` recovers and overshoots
- `6L` returns to strong, credible shrinkage

So the deepest untied run is the strongest trustworthy example of paper-style shrinkage in this single-seed slice.

## 5. Trusted Low-ML2R Cases: Where Shrinkage Is Most Convincing

Among final-checkpoint layer results with `MMCS >= 0.95`, the lowest ML2R values are:

| Architecture | Depth | Layer | MMCS | ML2R | Grouped top-3 ML2R | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| untied | 6 | linear2 | 0.961 | 0.019 | 1.013 | severe single-match failure, grouping rescues it |
| untied | 6 | hidden_layers.0 | 0.990 | 0.497 | 0.998 | strong shrinkage or splitting |
| untied | 6 | hidden_layers.1 | 0.992 | 0.604 | 0.981 | strong shrinkage / splitting |
| untied | 6 | hidden_layers.3 | 0.998 | 0.619 | 1.119 | strong single-match shrinkage, grouping over-recovers |
| untied | 6 | linear1 | 0.974 | 0.702 | 0.719 | strong shrinkage that persists under grouping |
| untied | 2 | linear1 | 0.999 | 0.884 | 1.078 | shrinkage mostly removed by grouping |
| tied | 5 | linear1 | 0.985 | 0.891 | 0.896 | strong and persistent shrinkage |

### Interpretation

This table is one of the most useful in the whole analysis because it separates:

- true persistent shrinkage
- shrinkage that may largely be a matching or splitting artifact

Two especially important distinctions:

1. `6L untied linear1` remains low even after grouped top-3 matching (`0.719`), so this looks like genuine matched norm loss rather than just a greedy matching artifact.
2. `6L untied linear2` has catastrophic top-1 ML2R (`0.019`) but grouped top-3 ML2R near `1.0`, which strongly suggests a feature-splitting or matching pathology rather than literal disappearance of the target mechanism.

That means the strongest persistent shrinkage case is not `linear2`; it is more plausibly `linear1` and some hidden layers in `6L untied`.

## 6. Temporal Onset: ML2R Shrinkage Appears Early, Raw Under-Scaling Never Does

### Result

Using the criterion:

- `MMCS >= 0.95`
- `ML2R < 0.90`

the onset heatmaps show that shrinkage flags appear early in many runs.

Important tied onsets:

- `4L tied hidden_layers.0`: `5k`
- `4L tied hidden_layers.1`: `5k`
- `5L tied linear1`: `5k`
- `6L tied linear1`: `5k`
- all `6L tied` hidden layers: `5k`

Important untied onsets:

- `2L untied linear1`: `5k`
- `3L untied linear1`: `20k`
- `4L untied linear1`: `5k`
- `5L untied linear1`: `20k`
- `6L untied linear1`: `5k`
- `6L untied hidden_layers.2`: `5k`
- `6L untied hidden_layers.0`: `20k`
- `6L untied hidden_layers.1`: `20k`
- `6L untied hidden_layers.3`: `15k`
- `6L untied linear2`: `30k`

Meanwhile:

- no raw under-scaling onset occurs anywhere at `raw_fro_ratio < 0.90`

### Interpretation

This temporal contrast is crucial. The analysis suggests that what we call shrinkage does not emerge as a late-stage collapse in raw learned scale. Instead, matched norm shrinkage can be present as early as `5k`, while raw layer norms remain healthy throughout.

That pushes the interpretation strongly toward:

- matching-space norm loss
- CI attenuation
- representational splitting

rather than raw factor collapse.

## 7. Matching Artifacts Versus Real Shrinkage

The grouped top-k matching plots are very useful for telling apart:

- “the best single matched component is too small”
- “the target mechanism is distributed across several components”

### Strong evidence for splitting or grouping artifacts

The clearest example is:

- `6L untied linear2`

At the final checkpoint:

- MMCS is high enough to treat the direction as meaningful
- top-1 ML2R is almost zero
- grouped top-3 ML2R is basically `1.0`

That is exactly the pattern expected if the single-best matched component is tiny, but a small set of learned components together reconstruct the target-scale mechanism.

There are milder versions of the same pattern in:

- `2L untied linear1`
- `3L untied linear1`
- `6L untied hidden_layers.3`

### Stronger evidence for real persistent shrinkage

The most convincing persistent shrinkage cases are those where grouped recovery does **not** fix the problem much:

- `5L tied linear1`
- `6L untied linear1`

These cases remain visibly low even when grouped recovery is used, so they are the strongest candidates for true matched norm shrinkage rather than a pure grouping artifact.

### Practical implication

This analysis validates the notebook design choice to keep matching last in the investigation order. If we had looked only at top-1 ML2R, we would have overstated shrinkage in some layers, especially `6L untied linear2`.

## 8. Tied Versus Untied: What Actually Differs

### Tied runs

The tied runs show a fairly clean regime up through `4L`:

- raw ratios near `1`
- layer-1 MMCS near `1`
- layer-1 ML2R near `1`
- CI attenuation in `linear1`, but modest hidden-layer attenuation

The tied system changes character at higher depth:

- `5L tied` shows a clear `linear1` shrinkage event
- `6L tied` shows widespread early shrinkage flags in hidden layers, stronger hidden attenuation, and pathological `linear1` overshoot rather than clean shrinkage

So tiedness does not simply prevent shrinkage at high depth. Instead, it appears to delay it and then transition into a more pathological regime.

### Untied runs

Untied runs look less stable and more distributed from the start:

- `2L untied linear1` already shows clear shrinkage
- `3L` to `5L` untied runs have strong CI attenuation across hidden layers
- delta usage is much stronger in untied runs
- `6L untied` concentrates the strongest trustworthy multi-layer shrinkage signal in the whole sweep

Untiedness seems to allow more redistribution:

- across hidden layers
- into delta
- and across multiple matched components

That flexibility may help preserve raw layer norms while still producing low single-match ML2R.

### Best summary statement

For this `rep1` analysis:

- tied runs are cleaner and more stable at shallow-to-mid depth
- untied runs show stronger CI attenuation and stronger deep matched shrinkage
- tied `6L` is abnormal, but not in the same way as untied `6L`

## 9. Functional Contraction: Secondary Evidence Only

### Stable cases

In stable final-checkpoint cases, raw output norm ratios are generally close to 1, with very small output MSE:

- tied `2L` to `5L`: raw output ratio around `0.94` to `0.95`
- untied `2L`, `4L`, `6L`: raw output ratio near `1.0`
- raw output MSE stays small, typically `1e-5` to `1e-4`

Masked outputs are usually more contracted than raw outputs:

- tied `2L` to `5L`: masked output ratio around `0.75` to `0.88`
- untied `2L`, `4L`, `6L`: masked output ratio around `0.81`, `0.81`, `0.97`

### Jacobians

The Jacobian results are mixed:

- tied runs often have masked Jacobian ratios greater than 1 in the stable slice
- untied runs tend to have masked Jacobian ratios well below 1

This suggests Jacobian behavior is more region-sensitive than the simpler layerwise norm analysis.

### Interpretation

Given the numerical instability in some denominator-based ratios, the functional analysis should not be treated as the primary evidence for shrinkage here. It is better used as a supporting layer:

- raw functional behavior is broadly preserved
- CI masking often worsens functional fidelity
- but the cleanest shrinkage diagnosis still comes from matching metrics and CI attenuation

## 10. What This Means For The Original Shrinkage Question

The original motivating question was roughly:

- do deeper tied and untied models exhibit shrinkage, especially in layer 1?

For this `rep1` slice, the answer is:

### Yes, but not in the simplest raw-norm sense

If shrinkage means:

- “raw reconstructed layers are too small”

then the answer is mostly **no**.

If shrinkage means:

- “matched subcomponents recover the right direction but not enough norm”

then the answer is **yes**, especially in:

- `2L untied linear1`
- `5L tied linear1`
- `6L untied linear1`
- multiple `6L untied` hidden layers

### The strongest driver of apparent scale loss is CI attenuation

Even where ML2R shrinkage is not dramatic, the CI-masked layers are significantly attenuated. This is especially true for `linear1`, where the attenuation gap is around `0.30` to `0.41` across nearly all runs.

### Deep untied is the strongest true shrinkage regime

Among trustworthy matched cases, `6L untied` stands out the most. It combines:

- high MMCS in several layers
- low ML2R in several layers
- persistent shrinkage after grouped matching in `linear1`
- stronger delta usage than most other runs

That combination is hard to explain away as a pure artifact.

### Deep tied is a different pathology

`6L tied` is not best described as “more shrinkage.” It looks more like:

- larger hidden-layer attenuation
- widespread early shrinkage flags
- but a pathological overshoot in `linear1` top-1 ML2R

That suggests tied `6L` is entering a qualitatively different regime from `5L tied`, not merely extending the same shrinkage curve.

## 11. Ranked Findings

### Strongest conclusions

1. Raw SPD layer norms are not collapsing. The decomposition remains near target scale across all runs.
2. CI attenuation is large, stable, and widespread, especially in `linear1`.
3. Trusted paper-style shrinkage is strongest in `6L untied`.
4. `5L tied linear1` is the clearest tied-run shrinkage case.
5. `6L untied linear2` is mostly a matching/splitting pathology rather than pure shrinkage, because grouped recovery restores norm.

### Moderate-confidence conclusions

1. Untied runs distribute scale more diffusely across hidden layers and delta.
2. Tied runs stay cleaner through `4L` and then degrade sharply at higher depth.
3. ML2R shrinkage often appears very early in training, long before any raw under-scaling is visible.

### Weakest conclusions

1. Any claim based on giant functional ratios in `6L tied` or masked `5L untied` should be treated cautiously.
2. Jacobian-based contraction is informative in some runs but not stable enough here to lead the story.

## Final Conclusion

For the `rep1` slice of `exp_07`, the shrinkage story is real but highly specific:

- it is **not** a broad raw-norm collapse
- it is **mostly** a combination of CI attenuation and selective matched norm loss
- it is **strongest and most trustworthy** in `6L untied`
- it appears in tied runs too, but the tied story changes at high depth from clean shrinkage into more pathological behavior

If I had to summarize the whole sweep in one sentence:

> The main effect is not that deep SPD reconstructions become uniformly too small, but that deep models, especially untied ones, preserve raw scale while losing matched feature norm through CI attenuation, residual redistribution, and selective shrinkage in the matched component space.
